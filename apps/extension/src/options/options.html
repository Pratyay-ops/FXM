FXM Connect

Version Development build

FXM Connect lets the FXM web app reach music services from your own browser. Account access is separate for Spotify and YouTube Music, off by default, and only enabled when you explicitly opt in below. Cookies and account credentials stay inside the extension; only music metadata is returned to FXM. It has no analytics or tracking.

Development build. It treats any app served on the localhost origins listed below as FXM, including other projects and their dependencies. Keep the Spotify login off for localhost, or use a separate browser profile for development. Never publish this build.

YouTube Music

Off by default. When on, FXM Connect can read the YouTube cookies needed to access the YouTube Music library you are signed in to in this browser. FXM Connect uses them only in its own background to request your music library and sends FXM only normalized music metadata. It never sends your cookies or account credentials to the FXM page or a server. The account reader uses YouTube's unofficial InnerTube interface, which may change and can stop working. FXM only reads your music library; it does not change your YouTube Music account.

Firefox may ask you to allow FXM Connect to handle authentication information when you enable account access. The extension uses the browser session locally to retrieve music metadata; no FXM server receives your cookies or credentials.

Spotify

Off by default. When on, the FXM app can ask FXM Connect for a Spotify access token for the account you are signed in to at open.spotify.com in this browser. The app asks for one only after you also connect your Spotify account in FXM. That account sync is experimental: it has been tested with a stand-in for Spotify, not yet with real accounts. FXM Connect fetches the token from open.spotify.com with your browser's existing Spotify session and passes FXM only the token response. It never reads, stores or shares your cookies. Spotify's terms don't permit automated access like FXM's, and they let Spotify suspend accounts that break them; no such case has been reported for this kind of use.

When you turn this on, Firefox asks you to let FXM Connect "share authentication information with extension developer", because the token is your Spotify login. Here that means the FXM web app in this browser, which uses the token only for its requests to Spotify; no FXM server receives it. If you withdraw the consent in Firefox's add-on settings, FXM can no longer use your login.

Development build only, off by default, and only in effect while the setting above is on. Any app on the localhost origins below could then get a token for your Spotify account.
