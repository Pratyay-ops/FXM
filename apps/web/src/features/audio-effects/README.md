# Audio effects (`features/audio-effects`)

Stage 8.1: the equalizer, loudness normalization, crossfade, playback speed and the sleep timer, in the web app (and the desktop and Android apps, which run it). The effects themselves are `@fxm/core`'s (`effects/`: bands, presets, curve, loudness gain) and `@fxm/player`'s (the player's crossfade and sleep timer, the engines' equalizer and levels); this folder holds the settings, the wiring and the UI. The player package's README has [how each effect works on each platform](../../../../../packages/player/README.md#audio-effects).

## What the user gets

- **Settings → Sound** (`effects-section.tsx`, anchor `#sound`):
  - **Equalizer:** a switch, and "Adjust the bands" (`EqualizerDisclosure`, a 44 px summary with a chevron): a preset menu (Flat, Bass boost, Rock, Vocal… or Custom once a band moved), the curve the filters play, one slider per band (31 Hz to 16 kHz, −12 to +12 dB in half decibels) and "Reset to flat". The band sliders fill from 0 dB in the middle (up for a boost, down for a cut, `RangeSlider`'s `origin="center"`, with a mark at 0 dB), keep their thumb visible (`appearance="setting"`), and fill in the muted colour while the equalizer is off, like the curve. Moving a band or picking a preset switches it on. When it can't process the track that plays, the row says why (`equalizerNote`): without FXM Connect (Lite, Free only) it can't read YouTube audio; in the desktop and Android apps not yet (stage 7.6; the Android app's native player); with FXM Connect only after a googlevideo redirect, until the next track.
  - **Normalize volume:** a switch (on by default), the level (Normal −14 LUFS, Quiet −18, Quietest −23) and track or album gain, with what it does to the track that plays ("This track plays 3.9 dB quieter"), part of the switch's description (not a live region, so it isn't read out on every track change).
  - **Crossfade:** 0 (off) to 12 s, its thumb always visible, and "Crossfade within albums" (off: albums stay gapless). Disabled where the engine has none (the Android app's native player), and where the device doesn't let FXM change the volume (iOS: `effectsSupport().volume`), each with its reason.
  - **Playback speed:** 0.5× to 2×, pitch kept.
  - The notes about the track that plays show only while a stream is loaded (`useEffectsNotes`): with nothing playing, or a restored track that hasn't resolved yet, there is no track to talk about.
  - Arriving at `#sound` (the Sound dialog's "More in Settings") moves the focus to the section's heading, so keyboard and screen reader users land there, not back on the button that opened the dialog.
- **The Sound dialog** (`sound-dialog.tsx`, loaded on first use): the sleep timer (Off, 15, 30, 45 min, 1 h, 1 h 30 min, End of track, with the time left), the speed at a tap, volume normalization, the equalizer with its bands behind "Adjust the bands", and "More in Settings". The timer's and the speed's chips are native radio groups named by their headings (one tab stop each, the arrow keys move and pick, forced colours mark the pick); picking the running timer's chip again starts it over. The countdown is a `timer`, which screen readers don't read out every second; each change of the setting is said once ("Sleep timer on: pauses in 30 minutes"). The app layout renders it once; it opens from the **player bar's Sound button**, from **Now playing's "Sound" shortcut** on phones (between Lyrics and Queue), and from **"Equalizer" in the track menus** (`equalizerActions`, the catalog pages' `EqualizerActions` seam, wired in `main.tsx`), which opens it at the equalizer: its part at the top, its switch focused, its bands open.
- **What differs from normal shows** wherever the dialog opens from: the Sound button and the shortcut show sliders, or a moon while a sleep timer runs, highlighted with a dot while the speed isn't 1×, the equalizer is on or a timer runs, and their names say which ("Sound: 1.25× speed, equalizer on, sleep timer on", `soundLabel`). The speed shows beside the bar's button, in place of the shortcut's label (also below 360 px), and in the mini player on phones (whose link names it too).
- **"Sleep timer paused playback"** in the app's toast when the timer paused something. A timer that ends while nothing plays ends quietly, in the player and in the setting.
- **With autoplay** (stage 3.4, [its README](../autoplay/README.md)): the timer has the last word. When an end-of-track timer stops the queue's last track, autoplay still adds its songs, but they wait for Play; it doesn't play on after any timer ended either. Crossfade fades into autoplay's songs as into any other.

## How it is wired

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `effects-store.ts` | `useAudioEffectsStore`: the `AudioEffectsSettings`, always valid (`sanitizeAudioEffects`), persisted under `fxm.audio-effects.v1` and followed across tabs (`syncAudioEffectsAcrossTabs`, from `App`); and the sleep timer (`{ endsAt, minutes }` or `{ endOfTrack }`), never stored: a reload ends it |
| `connect.ts` | `connectAudioEffects(player)`: gives a player the settings now and on every change, and the sleep timer; when the player's timer ends, the setting ends too, and when it paused something (`sleep`) the toast says so. `createAppEngine()`: the direct audio engine with `isReadableHere`, which is `isMediaReadable` with the connection's platform and capabilities (`features/connect/connection.ts`), read whenever a stream loads; `appEngineOptions()` holds that rule for the desktop app's engine too |
| `status.ts` | The words: why the equalizer can't process a track, what loudness does to it, and the formats of speeds, lengths, gains and the time left |
| `equalizer-editor.tsx`, `effects-section.tsx` | The equalizer's editor and Settings → Sound |
| `sound-dialog.tsx`, `sound-dialog-host.tsx`, `sound-dialog-store.ts` | The Sound dialog; the host renders it from the app layout only while it is open, and loads it the first time it opens, so it stays out of the app's first download. `openDialog('equalizer')` opens it at the equalizer |
| `sound-button.tsx` | The player bar's Sound button and Now playing's shortcut, which open it and show what differs from normal |
| `download-loudness.ts` | `attachFileLoudness`: a completed download keeps its stream's loudness (YouTube's `loudnessDb`); when the stream said none, the downloads runtime fills it in from the file's own ReplayGain or R128 tags (music-metadata, loaded on first use), changing the record only while it is still that completed file |
| `equalizer-actions.ts` | "Equalizer" in the track menus |

The app's playback (`features/now-playing/playback.ts`) creates its engine with `createAppEngine()` and connects `connectAudioEffects(player)` first, before anything listens to the player: applying the effects updates the player's state. In the desktop app the composition root builds that player with the desktop's engine instead (`setUpDesktopPlayback` in `src/app/services/desktop.ts`, stage 7.3), a direct audio engine with the same readability rule (`appEngineOptions()`), so the effects work there as in a browser, the equalizer on the user's files and the free sources; the stream lifecycle of the composition root (stage 2.9b) hands every stream over with the loudness its source gave. Local files play through that player too (stage 2.9), so their songs (always readable `blob:` URLs) get the equalizer, their ReplayGain level, crossfade, speed and the sleep timer. `connectAudioEffects` follows one player's timer: with several, the first to end would end the setting for all of them. The embeddable mini-player (`src/embed`, stage 5.2) keeps its own player, which none of this reaches: no settings, no Sound dialog and no sleep timer, only the player's default effects, loudness normalization turning loud tracks down through the element's volume.

## Tests

`effects-store.test.ts` (settings, presets, validation of stored data, the timer, tabs), `connect.test.ts` (a real `Player` on a `FakeEngine` following the store, the timer pausing it and the toast, a timer ending while nothing plays, an engine's own timer, disconnecting, readability by platform), `status.test.ts` (the notes, which name the free sources this build offers), and `effects-section.test.tsx` and `sound-dialog.test.tsx`, which render the app (`renderApp`) on the test playback (`setupTestPlayback`) and use the switches, menus, sliders and radio chips as people do, with the keyboard too (arrow keys, the focus on opening at the equalizer and on arriving at `#sound`), and check the buttons' names and what screen readers hear. In the composition root (`src/app/services`), `app-services.test.ts` plays a YouTube Music song at the loudness its recorded `/player` answer gives, and `desktop.test.ts` checks that the desktop's engine gets the readability rule.

**Live check in the composed app (2026-10-26).** The production build served by `vite preview` on 127.0.0.1, in Playwright's headless Chromium with `--mute-audio` and a temporary profile, on the browser tests' fake network (`e2e/fake-services.ts`: the recorded fixtures, WAV tones), on macOS 15.5 (arm64). With a fake FXM Connect, a YouTube Music song played at an element volume of 0.640, which is 10^(−3.88/20) from its `/player` answer's `loudnessDb`. The Sound dialog's 1.5× set `playbackRate` to 1.5 with `preservesPitch`. Bass boost moved the stream onto a `crossorigin` element in a running `AudioContext`, whose output an analyser read at 0.0140 RMS against 0.0431 at its input: the −3.88 dB loudness gain and the −6 dB headroom, to within 0.1 dB. "More in Settings" then focused the heading of Settings → Sound, the second of the page's eleven sections. Without FXM Connect, an Internet Archive track went through the equalizer the same way (0.043 RMS in, 0.022 out), and its notes said it carries no loudness. The browser tests `e2e/playback.spec.ts` and `e2e/downloads.spec.ts` passed on the same build.
