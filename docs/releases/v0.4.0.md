FXM 0.4.0 is the second release of FXM, a free and open-source music player that runs in your browser: open the web app at https://fxmusic.pages.dev/app/, or, in most browsers, install it as an app that opens in its own window. There is nothing to sign up for and no FXM server: your library, history and settings stay on your device unless you turn on sync, and the songs you play go to Last.fm or ListenBrainz only if you connect an account for scrobbling. This release adds autoplay, an experimental sync of your Spotify account, saving downloads in the format you choose, sound settings, plugins, and a player you can put on your own website.

FXM is in early development: what isn't released yet is listed under "Coming soon" below.

## Get it

- **The web app** at https://fxmusic.pages.dev/app/, which most browsers can install as an app. If you have it open when this release comes out, it finds the new version within about an hour and says "A new version of FXM is ready": choose Reload, or it starts the next time you open FXM.
- **FXM Connect 0.4.0**, the small browser extension that lets the web app reach YouTube Music and Spotify from your own browser, on a computer. For Chrome, Edge, Brave and other Chromium browsers, download `fxm-connect-chromium-0.4.0.zip` below and load it unpacked, as [its install steps](https://github.com/Pratyay-ops/FXM/blob/v0.4.0/apps/extension/README.md#install) describe. Keep Developer mode on, because Chrome turns unpacked extensions off without it. Firefox can load `fxm-connect-firefox-0.4.0.zip` as a temporary add-on, which Firefox removes when it restarts.
- **Updating FXM Connect 0.1.0.** FXM Connect doesn't update itself: unzip `fxm-connect-chromium-0.4.0.zip` over the same folder you loaded 0.1.0 from, then press Reload on its card on the browser's extensions page (`chrome://extensions`, `edge://extensions` or `brave://extensions`). The only change in 0.4.0 is the text of its options page: 0.1.0's says that the Spotify login setting changes nothing, which is no longer true, because the Spotify account sync below uses it. Otherwise 0.1.0 works with this release as it is.

## New in 0.4.0

- **Autoplay.** When your queue runs low, FXM adds songs like the ones you just played, so the music keeps going, and the songs you like and skip steer what comes next. The songs it adds are marked Autoplay in the queue, where you can remove them. It's on by default: switch it off in Settings → Playback, or under Next up in the queue. Autoplay needs YouTube Music, with FXM Connect or in the experimental Lite tier; with FXM Connect, Spotify's radio helps pick the songs while Spotify is on.
- **Spotify account sync, experimental.** With FXM Connect, connect your Spotify account in Settings → Spotify account, and FXM keeps a read-only copy of your liked songs and playlists in your library, and Spotify's home for you on FXM's Home page. It syncs again about every six hours while FXM is open, and whenever you choose Sync now. FXM never changes anything in your Spotify account. Disconnect stops the sync and forgets your login's token at once, and the copies stay in your library unless you choose to remove them. The sync is off until you connect, and it needs FXM Connect's "Use my Spotify login from this browser" option. **It is experimental: it has been tested with a stand-in for Spotify, not yet with a real Spotify account.** If it fails, Settings says why, and FXM never empties a copy because of an answer it couldn't read; please tell us what happened on the [issue tracker](https://github.com/Pratyay-ops/FXM/issues). Before you connect, a notice says what FXM reads and keeps, and that Spotify's User Guidelines forbid automated access, with the suspension of the account as the remedy; no such case is known for this kind of read-only use. If you sync your library with GitHub, the copies go to your sync gist too, your private playlists included.
- **Save downloads in the format you want.** A downloaded song's menu has Save to device…: save the file as it is, or converted on your device to FLAC, ALAC, WAV, MP3, AAC or Opus, with its tags, cover art and lyrics. Each format says what you get. YouTube Music's audio is Opus or AAC and never lossless, so saving it as FLAC says that you get a bigger file, not a better one, and the file remembers it: opened in Local files, it shows as converted from its source, such as Opus, never as lossless. The converter loads from FXM's site the first time a save needs it, as a file of its own, and stays on your device for saving offline. Your downloads themselves stay the file their source has.
- **Sound.** The player bar's Sound button and Settings → Sound have an equalizer with presets, volume normalization (on by default, so loud and quiet songs play at an even level when their source says how loud they are), crossfade of up to 12 seconds, playback speed from 0.5× to 2× without changing the pitch, and a sleep timer. The equalizer works where FXM can read the audio: your own files, Internet Archive, and YouTube Music with FXM Connect, but not in the Lite tier. Settings says when an effect can't work here.
- **Plugins.** Add one in Settings → Plugins, from an address or a file you choose: its catalog joins search, and its songs play through it. FXM ships no plugins and suggests none. Before anything is installed, FXM shows every address the plugin can contact, its checksum and where it came from. Each plugin runs apart from FXM's pages and your data, and every update waits for your approval. Plugins work in the web app, not yet in Safari or on iPhone and iPad.
- **A player for your website.** On the page of an album or a playlist from YouTube Music, YouTube or Internet Archive, open More options and choose Share. Pick the card or the compact layout and a light, dark or automatic theme, then copy the embed code into your page, or copy the player's link. Your visitors play it in their own browser: Internet Archive's music everywhere, and YouTube Music in the experimental Lite tier, where their browser allows it. As soon as your page shows the player, each visitor's browser loads its details and cover art from its source, such as YouTube, so mention it in your site's privacy notice.

## Also in this release

- **Spotify in the web app.** With FXM Connect, Spotify search and related artists now work in the browser, as 0.1.0 promised: 0.1.0 never turned Spotify on there, and Settings said that this version couldn't read it.
- **Better recommendations on Home.** A song from Spotify or an import now starts YouTube Music's radio only once FXM is sure which song it is, so one uncertain match no longer fills Discover and "Because you listened to" with unrelated music for a week.
- **Spotify off means no Spotify at all.** With Spotify switched off in Settings, FXM no longer loads covers from Spotify's image servers: Spotify songs in your library show a placeholder instead, and Save to device saves them without a cover.
- **Security updates** for the tools that build FXM: two advisories are fixed, neither of which FXM's use of those tools could reach, and every dependency is now checked for advisories every week.
- **The converter's source code.** The converter is FXM's own audio-only build of FFmpeg, with LAME for MP3 and Opus, under the LGPL, and ships as a separate file next to the web app. The exact source code it was built from is attached to this release as `fxm-ffmpeg-core-source-0.4.0.zip`, and FXM's site serves the same files in `/source/ffmpeg-core/`, on the same server as the converter; the [third-party notices](https://fxmusic.pages.dev/third-party-notices.txt) list each one.
